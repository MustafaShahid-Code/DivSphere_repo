A new system's success is decided less by its feature set than by whether the people using it every day genuinely understand it — not just where the buttons are, but how to handle the judgment calls that generic training never covers.

## What's included

Role-based training designed around what each team actually needs to do differently, not a single generic walkthrough delivered to everyone regardless of role. Both on-site and remote delivery, structured to fit how your team actually works. Training that continues past the initial rollout, since the questions that come up three weeks in — once people are handling real, ambiguous situations rather than a training scenario — are usually the ones that matter most. Genuinely well-prepared super-users, equipped to field the department-specific questions that come up daily, with a clear path to escalate what even they can't answer. And technical services delivered in line with applicable local licensing requirements, so training and support are structured correctly for where they're actually delivered.

## When to bring us in

Plan training as part of the project from the start, not as an afterthought scheduled the week before go-live — training that only covers "how to click the buttons" is consistently one of the most common, avoidable reasons rollouts stall.
