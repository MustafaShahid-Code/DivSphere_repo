Shipping a mobile app is the easy part. Keeping it working through the next five OS updates, the next round of App Store review guideline changes, and real users on real devices with patchy connectivity — that's where most mobile projects actually live or die.

## What's included

An honest native-vs-cross-platform recommendation based on your actual requirements — deep platform-specific features and maximum performance point toward native; a smaller team maintaining one codebase across iOS and Android points toward cross-platform frameworks like React Native or Flutter. Neither is universally right, and a vendor that recommends the same stack for every client isn't actually evaluating your case. Offline-first design where connectivity can't be guaranteed, since a mobile app that only works with a perfect connection fails exactly when users need it most. App Store and Play Store submission handled end-to-end, including the review-guideline details that cause first-submission rejections. And a maintenance plan built around the reality that both platforms push OS updates every year, each capable of breaking something that worked fine the day before.

## When to bring us in

If mobile is the primary way customers will interact with the product, or if a mobile app needs to extend a system you already run, involve us before the platform decision is locked in — it's the single hardest choice to reverse later.
